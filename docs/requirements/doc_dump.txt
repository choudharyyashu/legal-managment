0: Legal Management System Design Requirements
1: Required design and workflow scope for the current development stage
2: I want the current design expanded into a complete blueprint for a law firm platform serving 50 attorneys and staff combined, with capacity to grow beyond 50. The blueprint must account for the depth of practice management associated with Clio, configurable workflows associated with Filevine, bankruptcy preparation associated with Best Case, immigration preparation associated with Docketwise, and integrated law firm accounting capable of eventually replacing CosmoLex.
3: This document addresses what must be designed and specified now: the workflows, screens, related records, calculations, permissions, integration methods, and implementation dependencies. Production connections and completed accounting do not need to be represented as already delivered. The current design must make room for them and show how they will work together.
4: 1  Shared structure across the firm
5: Client and matter records. One client may have multiple matters; one matter may involve multiple clients and related parties. Separate people, organizations, relationships, contact details, communication preferences, representation dates, conflict status, engagement terms, and matter-specific information. Support related and consolidated cases without merging their billing, permissions, or deadlines.
6: Matter structure. Include unique matter numbers, practice and matter subtype, jurisdiction, court, judge, case number, opposing counsel, responsible attorney, team, fee arrangement, stage, custom fields, important dates, related proceedings, and closure status. Support plaintiff and defendant roles and different workflows within the same practice area.
7: Connected records. Design reusable records for parties, insurers, providers, witnesses, experts, creditors, beneficiaries, dependents, employers, lienholders, courts, vendors, and referral sources. Preserve each participant’s role within each matter. Avoid separate duplicate contact databases inside practice modules.
8: Matter workspace. Provide a matter overview, activity timeline, communications, documents, tasks, deadlines, evidence, participants, time, expenses, billing, trust, and a practice-specific workspace. Display next actions, missing information, unresolved requests, responsible staff, and items awaiting attorney approval.
9: Configurable workflow engine. Specify stages, entry and exit conditions, required information, automatic assignments, task dependencies, conditional branches, parallel tasks, relative deadlines, reminders, escalation, pause/reopen handling, and execution history. Administrators need an editor for routine changes and version control for published workflows.
10: 2  Common intake and engagement
11: Inquiry → prospect record → related-party collection → recorded conflict review → consultation → fee and scope approval → engagement document → signature tracking → initial payment where applicable → matter creation → team assignment → practice workflow. Include declined, pending, referred, duplicate, and withdrawn branches. Questionnaire answers should populate verified records, with review of conflicting answers rather than automatic overwriting.
13: Litigation Design From Intake Through Pleadings
14: Civil and commercial litigation need their own substantive workspace and configurable plaintiff, defendant, and related-proceeding paths. The same litigation tools must also be available when a PI, probate, family, or other matter becomes contested.
15: 3  Assessment and prefiling work
16: Case assessment. Record claims, counterclaims, defenses, requested remedies, factual chronology, key documents, damage categories, estimated exposure, responsible parties, insurance, litigation objectives, and settlement authority. Create an issues and proof matrix connecting each issue to supporting evidence, missing evidence, witnesses, and assigned research or investigation.
17: Preservation and investigation. Design litigation-hold preparation, recipients, acknowledgments, reminders, custodians, devices, data sources, preservation requests, document collection, witness interviews, investigators, and evidence review. Flag missing collections and distinguish material received from material reviewed.
18: Demand and early resolution. Support demand letters, response deadlines, proposed settlement terms, negotiation history, releases, payment terms, and completion tasks. Unresolved demands should branch into filing preparation; resolved demands should create performance and closure tasks.
19: Prefiling requirements. Provide configurable checklists for jurisdiction, venue, service planning, agreements requiring notice or ADR, prerequisites selected by the attorney, limitations analysis, parties, standing, and requested relief. Each legal determination needs an owner, source, review status, and attorney decision.
20: 4  Pleadings and service
21: Document preparation. Plan complaint, petition, answer, counterclaim, crossclaim, third-party complaint, amended pleadings, summons, exhibits, declarations, and proposed orders. Templates must handle multiple parties, conditional allegations, signatures, captions, and version history. Link allegations and exhibits to their source material.
22: Filing workflow. Draft → internal review → attorney approval → filing package → submission by the supported method → acceptance or rejection → filed copy and receipt → service tasks. Track submission separately from acceptance; rejected filings need visible correction and resubmission tasks.
23: Party-specific service. Track service separately for every party: documents served, method, address, process server, attempts, actual service date, proof, waiver, defects, and follow-up. Record responses and extensions by party rather than assigning one universal answer deadline to the matter.
24: Defensive intake. Allow a served pleading to open the workflow: capture service evidence, proposed response deadline, representation scope, insurer notice, response strategy, and required appearances. Include default-risk escalation and attorney review of disputed service dates.
25: 5  Court events and scheduling
26: Design court-notice and order intake that proposes linked hearings, response tasks, and deadlines for review. Store the triggering event, source document, applicable rule or order, calculation method, extensions, reviewer, and approval. Changes to a hearing, trial, or service date must display affected dates before a reviewer accepts recalculation. Track stays, amended scheduling orders, reassignment, transfer, removal, consolidation, and reopening without losing the original history.
28: Litigation Design Through Discovery Trial and Enforcement
29: 6  Discovery management
30: Written discovery. Maintain separate outgoing and incoming requests for interrogatories, requests for production, admissions, disclosures, and supplements. Each request needs service evidence, response date, extensions, assigned drafter, reviewer, response status, objections, documents requested, deficiencies, and follow-up. Support request-by-request review rather than one completed checkbox for an entire discovery set.
31: Production and evidence. Design original-file preservation, metadata, searchable OCR, duplicate detection, tagging, Bates numbering, production sets, redactions, privilege review, privilege logs, confidentiality designations, and protective-order restrictions. Track what was received, reviewed, withheld, produced, supplemented, or clawed back, and retain the exact production version.
32: Discovery disputes. Identify incomplete responses, missing documents, overdue requests, and unfulfilled commitments. Link meet-and-confer correspondence, calls, deficiency letters, agreed extensions, statements of discovery issues, motions to compel, hearings, orders, and compliance tasks. Keep disputed requests connected to the dispute and eventual resolution.
33: Subpoenas. Track recipient identity, documents or testimony requested, issue and service status, compliance date, objections, costs, production, confidentiality, privilege, enforcement, and return. Support third-party records and testimony, including vendor follow-up and incomplete returns.
34: Depositions and experts. Maintain witness type, availability, notices, subpoenas, location or video link, accommodation requests, exhibits, outlines, transcripts, video, errata, invoice, summaries, and follow-up discovery. Expert records need engagement, conflicts, qualifications, opinions, materials reviewed, disclosures, reports, deposition, and trial scheduling.
35: 7  Motions and hearings
36: For every motion, track relief requested, issues, supporting evidence, drafting assignments, response and reply tasks, service, hearing, proposed order, ruling, and resulting obligations. Support emergency applications, temporary relief, dispositive motions, evidentiary motions, continuances, and postjudgment motions. Hearing preparation should assemble the operative pleadings, briefs, exhibits, authorities, witness information, outline, and unresolved questions into a reviewable package.
37: 8  Settlement trial and postjudgment work
38: Settlement. Track mediation preparation, mediator, statements, offers, counteroffers, client authority, material terms, settlement document versions, signatures, payment, releases, dismissal, liens where applicable, and follow-up on unperformed terms.
39: Trial. Create a trial plan with witness and exhibit lists, objections, stipulations, deposition designations, motions in limine, jury or bench materials, instructions, verdict forms, subpoenas, demonstratives, exhibit binders, daily assignments, and preservation notes. Track admission and exclusion decisions and relevant transcript references.
40: Judgment and enforcement. Store judgment terms, entry and notice, awards, costs, fees, interest inputs, payment history, satisfaction, collection strategy, supplementary proceedings, liens, writs, and other attorney-selected enforcement steps. Create appeal and postjudgment review tasks linked to the underlying record.
41: Litigation dashboard. Show approaching deadlines, unserved parties, unanswered discovery, incomplete productions, pending motions, upcoming depositions and hearings, expert obligations, trial readiness, and matters stalled for a specific person or item.
43: Personal Injury Design For Intake Treatment and Records
44: 9  Claim setup and investigation
45: Inquiry → conflict and engagement → incident investigation → insurance and claim setup → treatment and evidence collection → demand readiness review → negotiation or litigation → settlement and disbursement → closure. Provide separate paths for different incident types and for claims entering the firm after suit has already been filed.
46: Incident and liability. Capture incident date and location, mechanism, parties, vehicles or property where applicable, injuries, police or incident reports, citations, photos, videos, scene information, witnesses, preservation needs, and disputed liability. Track comparative-fault issues, multiple defendants, related claimants, and liability evidence for attorney review.
47: Client and damages. Include relevant medical history, prior and later incidents, injuries by body area, treating providers, employment, missed work, wage evidence, functional limitations, out-of-pocket losses, future-care evidence, dependents, and client updates. Preserve source documents and distinguish client reports from verified records.
48: Insurance. Provide multiple insurers and coverages per matter, insureds, claimants, claim numbers, adjusters, representation notices, policy documents, limits, coverage questions, medical-payment or PIP activity, UM/UIM where applicable, reservation letters, payments, and remaining issues. Separate confirmed policy information from requested or disputed coverage.
49: 10  Treatment and medical records workflow
50: Treatment tracking. Maintain a provider directory linked to each client’s treatment episodes, facilities, dates of service, appointments, referrals, treatment status, balances, restrictions, and follow-up. Show the last client contact and outstanding treatment update; support ongoing care and interrupted care without automatically treating either as demand-ready.
51: Authorizations and requests. Prepare provider-specific authorizations and record requests using verified client information. Track signature, expiration or scope restrictions, destination, date range, records versus bills, delivery method, confirmation, vendor, quoted fee, payment, response date, and follow-up schedule.
52: Request status. Use requested, awaiting authorization, awaiting payment, processing, partially received, received, deficient, rejected, and closed states. A closed or rejected request must create a review task rather than disappear. Track missing dates of service, imaging, itemized statements, reports, and other requested components separately.
53: Records intake. Specify secure receipt from portals, email, approved vendors, uploads, or supported imports. Match the client, matter, provider, and request; preserve originals; scan files; OCR; index; identify duplicates; record service-date coverage; and flag unreadable, incomplete, or misassigned records for review.
54: Medical workspace. Design treatment chronology, diagnosis and procedure records, medical expenses, payments, adjustments, balances, records available, missing records, and source-linked summaries. AI suggestions need visible source references, review status, and a correction process.
56: Personal Injury Design For Demands Liens and Recovery
57: 11  Outside services and demand preparation
58: Vendor assignments. Design secure transfer of selected records and bills to approved medical-record retrieval, chronology, demand preparation, bill-review, lien-resolution, investigator, and expert companies. Specify package contents, authorization, recipient, permissions, delivery confirmation, turnaround target, fees, follow-up, and returned-work review.
59: Returned work. Link vendor chronologies, reports, drafts, and corrected records back to the matter and source documents. Support revisions and reassignments; preserve the package actually sent and the returned version. Identify the proposed API, portal, upload, or export route for each service rather than assuming every vendor has an integration.
60: Demand readiness. Provide a checklist for liability evidence, treatment status, complete medical records and bills, wage loss, future damages, insurance information, liens, client objectives, and attorney approval. Identify missing prerequisites and who must resolve each one.
61: Demand package. Generate a reviewable narrative, damages presentation, chronology, exhibits, supporting bills and records, and index. Track attorney edits, approved amount, client authority, version, recipients, delivery, response date, and follow-up. Support revised demands and multiple insurers or defendants.
62: 12  Negotiation and litigation
63: Track each offer and counteroffer with date, party, amount, terms, outstanding conditions, authority, and follow-up. Preserve substantive communications and proposed net recovery. A rejected offer or failed negotiation should create the selected next action. If suit is filed, activate the complete litigation workflow within the existing PI matter, retaining treatment, insurance, records, liens, damages, and negotiations.
64: 13  Liens settlement and disbursement
65: Lien register. Track lienholders and reimbursement claimants, asserted basis, requested documentation, original and verified amounts, disputed amounts, negotiations, reductions, written payoff confirmation, expiration where relevant, payment instructions, and release status. Assign unresolved items and keep proposed figures separate from approved payoffs.
66: Settlement model. Model gross recovery, fee agreement and calculation, case expenses, liens, third-party payments, prior advances or payments where applicable, reserves, and client proceeds. Allow multiple recoveries, revised assumptions, disputed funds, and staged distributions. Preserve calculation versions and approvals.
67: Closing workflow. Settlement approval → executed release and other required documents → proceeds received → bank availability confirmed → final lien and expense review → approved settlement statement → authorized disbursements → payment confirmation → remaining trust reconciliation → dismissal or other closure action → archive. Track every uncompleted condition.
68: PI dashboard. Show treatment updates due, requests awaiting action, missing records, incomplete vendor work, demands awaiting response, negotiation inactivity, unresolved liens, settlement funds pending, disbursements awaiting approval, and client updates due. Separate staff productivity from attorney decision queues.
70: Bankruptcy Preparation Design
71: Design a substantive bankruptcy workspace using Best Case as the functional reference. A chapter selector, generic questionnaire, or PDF upload screen does not define the required preparation process. The design must identify supported chapters, districts, form editions, calculations, and filing methods.
72: 14  Intake and financial information
73: Matter paths. Provide separate Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 paths and a clearly scoped Chapter 11 path if included. Distinguish individual, joint-debtor, and business requirements. Track prior filings, case numbers, relevant dates, household, residence history, exemptions for attorney selection, consultation, engagement, and initial document requests.
74: Debtor information. Design linked debtor, joint debtor, dependent, employer, business, creditor, codebtor, and related-party records. Include identity and addresses, household information, income sources, employment history, benefits, regular and irregular receipts, expenses, assets, ownership interests, secured property, leases, contracts, transfers, payments, lawsuits, and other financial history.
75: Creditor and debt records. Record creditor identity, notice address, account reference, claim amount, claim classification, security, collateral, arrears, priority issues, disputed or contingent status, codebtors, source evidence, and review status. Support duplicate detection, multiple addresses, amendments, and creditor-matrix preparation.
76: Document collection. Track pay records, tax returns, account statements, valuation material, debt evidence, judgments, business records, certificates, and attorney-selected supporting documents. Show requested period, due date, receipt, deficiencies, verification, and reminders. Do not treat an upload as attorney verification.
77: 15  Calculations forms and filing package
78: Calculation design. Specify means-test inputs, date-sensitive data, household assumptions, income periods, exclusions, expense categories, exemptions, asset values, secured claims, and Chapter 13 plan inputs. Each calculated result needs traceable inputs, source/version information, explanation, attorney review, and a controlled override with reason.
79: Form mapping. Provide a form inventory listing chapter, form number, edition, district, local form, data source fields, repeating schedules, calculation links, signatures, validation, and output format. Enter verified data once and propagate approved changes to affected forms while identifying documents that need regeneration.
80: Review and filing. Questionnaires → document verification → calculations → draft forms → inconsistency review → attorney review → client review and signatures → final package → supported filing process → accepted filing or rejection correction. Explicitly distinguish preparing a package from submitting it to a court system.
81: 16  Administration after filing
82: Plan records for case number, filing date, trustee, notices, creditor meetings, hearings, deficiencies, objections, amendments, plan confirmation, plan obligations, payment reporting, discharge, dismissal, conversion, reopening, and closure. Court notices should propose tasks and dates for review. Design versioned updates to forms and calculation data, with affected matters identified and reviewable before changes are applied.
84: Immigration Preparation Design
85: Design a substantive immigration workspace using Docketwise as the functional reference. Define supported case types and form packages rather than treating immigration as a generic matter category. The blueprint must show reusable questionnaires, linked people, form mapping, validation, supporting evidence, and ongoing case administration.
86: 17  Parties questionnaires and reusable information
87: Linked participants. Provide petitioner, beneficiary, sponsor, household member, dependent, employer, interpreter, preparer, and attorney roles. One person may hold different roles in different filings. Preserve family and employment relationships, shared addresses, identity records, and matter-specific facts without overwriting another case’s verified information.
88: Questionnaires. Design multilingual client-facing questionnaires, conditional questions, repeatable histories, save-and-return, assignments to different participants, document requests, reminders, staff assistance, translation review, and verification. Support missing, unknown, not applicable, declined, and inconsistent answers distinctly.
89: Immigration history. Plan structured records for names and aliases, addresses, employment, education, family, citizenship, identification documents, entries and exits, travel, admissions, status history, receipt and registration numbers, prior applications, proceedings, and attorney-reviewed eligibility or inadmissibility issues. Restrict sensitive information by role and matter.
90: 18  Forms evidence and filing
91: Package inventory. Identify supported family, employment, naturalization, humanitarian, consular, and removal-related workflows as applicable. For each supported package, list USCIS, EOIR, DOS, or DOL forms, form editions, required records, repeating sections, supplemental pages, signatures, fees, and supported submission steps. Mark unsupported packages and proposed specialized-software dependencies explicitly.
92: Smart form preparation. Map verified answers across multiple forms, validate missing or conflicting information, flag signature gaps, preserve form versions, and identify which forms become stale after a data change. Provide review screens showing the original answer, mapped field, calculated or derived value, and any attorney correction.
93: Evidence and assembly. Use case-specific document checklists, translations and certification records, financial and relationship evidence, client uploads, missing-item reminders, review decisions, cover letters, exhibits, indexes, and final package assembly. Track what has been approved and which version was included in the filed package.
94: Filing path. Preparation → staff quality review → attorney review → client confirmation and signatures → fee and filing-method confirmation → submission through the supported process → receipt or rejection → correction and follow-up. Identify steps requiring the client or attorney to act directly.
95: 19  Case administration and maintenance
96: Design receipt tracking, supported status updates, biometrics, interviews, requests for evidence, notices of intent, approvals, denials, expiration dates, renewals, work authorization, dependent timelines, and follow-up. An incoming notice must link to its matter and generate reviewable tasks. Maintain a form-edition and filing-instruction update process, identifying pending packages affected by changes. Status simulators and proposed connectors must remain distinguishable from actual agency information.
98: Probate Trust Administration and Family Law Design
99: 20  Probate trusts and estate administration
100: Separate matter paths. Design informal and formal probate, contested estates, trust administration, trust disputes, and related property or business issues. Estate planning documents may be an additional workflow; they must not replace administration and contested-probate functionality.
101: Intake and authority. Capture decedent, death information, domicile, wills, codicils, trusts, amendments, fiduciaries, heirs, beneficiaries, interested parties, disputes, property, businesses, existing proceedings, and representation scope. Track appointment, letters or other authority, bond requirements selected by counsel, notices, service, and objections.
102: Administration. Maintain an asset inventory with ownership, valuation date, liens, income, expenses, possession, sale status, and supporting records. Track creditor notices and claims, account access, tax-information requests, professionals, property sales, approvals, and required filings. Separate estate or trust money from the firm’s own operating and client trust accounting.
103: Beneficiary and distribution records. Design entitlement records, proposed allocations, disputed amounts, reserves, receipts, releases, court approvals, partial distributions, final distributions, and supporting calculations. Preserve the authority and approval for each distribution and any unresolved condition.
104: Contested proceedings and closure. Connect objections, petitions, discovery, motions, evidentiary hearings, mediation, settlement, and appeals to the full litigation tools. Administration closure needs final accounting, remaining obligations, distribution reconciliation, discharge or closing steps, document retention, and fiduciary follow-up.

107: Initial proceedings. Engagement → petition or defensive response → service → temporary-relief review → disclosures and financial-document collection → discovery and valuation → settlement or trial. Track temporary orders, evaluations, experts, mediation, hearing preparation, and order compliance.
108: Financial and parenting workspaces. Provide property and debt schedules, valuation evidence, proposed divisions, support inputs with versioned calculations and attorney review, parenting proposals, calendars, disputed issues, and comparison of settlement positions. Link every significant number or factual assertion to its supporting material.
109: Orders and follow-up. Design decree and agreement preparation, review, signatures, entry, property transfers, refinancing, retirement-order referrals, payment obligations, deadlines, compliance, modifications, and enforcement. Restrict access to sensitive records and respect recorded communication or contact restrictions.

122: Proceedings. Track payments, disputed charges, occupancy changes, stipulations, and compliance separately.
123: Execution and collections. 125: Automation Communications and Time Review Design
126: 25  Workflow configuration and deadline design
127: Reusable automation. Specify triggers for matter creation, stage changes, incoming documents, questionnaire completion, court events, payments, approvals, and elapsed time. Actions may create tasks, request documents, prepare drafts, suggest dates, alert a user, or propose a stage change. External messages, filings, financial actions, and legal decisions need the approval controls appropriate to the action.
128: Workflow editor. Design practice-specific templates with field definitions, conditions, dependencies, parallel branches, assigned roles, deadlines, escalation, required approvals, and completion rules. Show which workflow version applies to each matter and how existing matters are handled when the template changes.
129: Exceptions. Include failed actions, missing information, unavailable assignees, rejected approvals, cancelled events, reopened matters, stayed proceedings, duplicate events, and integration outages. Provide an automation history with inputs, action, result, responsible user, retry status, and resolution. Repeated processing must not recreate the same task or financial entry.
130: 26  Communications documents and daily work
131: Outlook and court notices. Design email, attachments, shared mailbox handling, conversation threading, matter suggestions, unmatched-item queues, and reviewed extraction of dates and tasks. Judicialink and other court sources need a documented access method. Keep receipt, review, calendar entry, and completion as separate states.
132: Existing phones and texts. Specify the proposed capture method for calls and SMS/MMS/iMessage from existing iPhone numbers and normal messaging apps. Address historical access, attachments, participants, threads, required Macs or iPhones, syncing, permissions, interruptions, and technical limits. Identify what can be demonstrated with the actual setup before promising coverage.
133: Documents and search. Plan Dropbox and OneDrive folder mapping, permissions, original files, version history, OCR, full-text search, templates, repeating parties, conditional content, redaction, signatures, and filing packages. Search results must respect matter permissions and link to the current record and relevant original.
134: 27  Automatic capture and review
135: Design Timing integration or an agreed better tracker for accessible document, email, browser, meeting, call, and text activity. Preserve original source identifiers, actual timestamps and durations, proposed matter, supporting evidence, proposed narrative, billed-time adjustment, reviewer, approval, and posting result. Do not derive time spent reading or writing a text solely from message timestamps.
136: Include every accessible individual call and text activity in the ordinary daily review. Keep uncertain matches unresolved rather than omitting them. Keep calls separate and permit text grouping when directed. Show idle periods, overlap, duplicates, inaccessible sources, rejected entries, corrections, and retries. Plan reviews at noon, 4 p.m., 6 p.m., and 9 p.m. Mountain time, with notification and practical approval on Mac and iPhone.
138: Integrated Billing Accounting and Settlement Design
139: The financial design must support independent billing, operating accounting, client trust accounting, and settlement disbursements. CosmoLex may remain the official record during transition. The architecture must allow its eventual removal without losing essential financial functions or history.
140: 28  Billing and operating accounting
141: Fee arrangements. Model hourly, flat-fee, contingency, mixed or phased arrangements, multiple timekeepers, attorney and matter rates, rate changes, expenses, advances, no-charge work, discounts, credits, write-offs, retainers, and allocation rules. Preserve the arrangement and rate used for each finalized transaction.
142: Billing workflow. Captured or entered work → proposed time and expenses → review → approval → draft invoice → attorney correction → final invoice → delivery → payment allocation → receivables follow-up. Support partial payments, refunds, disputed invoices, reversals, statements, aging, and approval history. Prevent repeated imports or posting attempts from creating duplicate charges.
143: Accounting structure. Plan chart of accounts, double-entry journals, operating bank accounts, receipts, disbursements, vendors, bills, reimbursements, bank imports, reconciliation, period controls, and financial reports. Define the journal treatment for each billing, payment, expense, refund, trust transfer, and settlement event, including reversal behavior.
144: Reporting. Specify general ledger, trial balance, balance sheet, income statement, cash reports, receivables aging, unbilled work, timekeeper and matter reports, fees and costs collected, and source-to-report drill-down. Define reporting basis and filters with the firm’s accounting adviser.
145: 29  Trust accounts and disbursement controls
146: Client ownership of funds. Design separate trust bank accounts, trust cash journals, and individual client/matter ledgers. Record payer, beneficiary, matter, purpose, source transaction, deposit status, bank availability, holds, authorized use, and running balances. Support multiple clients or matters without allowing one client’s funds to cover another client’s obligation.
147: Transaction workflow. Receipt → allocation → bank-availability review → permitted payment or transfer request → supporting-document review → approval → execution → confirmation → reconciliation. Plan returned deposits, failed payments, fees, interest handling, split allocations, refunds, disputed funds, reserves, and unclaimed balances for adviser review.
148: Ledger integrity. Define controls against insufficient client funds, concurrent disbursements, duplicate transactions, silent edits, and deletion of posted history. Corrections need traceable reversals. Financial permissions must separate preparation, approval, execution, and reconciliation where assigned by the firm.
149: Reconciliation. Specify bank-statement import, outstanding checks and deposits, matching, adjustments, discrepancies, and three-way comparison of adjusted bank balance, trust book balance, and total client-ledger balances. Preserve period reports, reviewer, supporting records, unresolved differences, and reopening history.
150: Settlement and migration links. Connect PI and other approved settlement statements to actual deposits, expenses, payoffs, fees, reserves, and client payments. Map CosmoLex balances, invoices, unbilled work, historical transactions, and outstanding items into a test migration plan with reconciliation and exception reports.
152: Client Portal Firm Dashboards and Interface Design

160: Management reporting.
